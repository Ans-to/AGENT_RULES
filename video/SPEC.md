# Director's production plan

**Goal:** Explain how a historical arrangement evolved into a self-reinforcing financial network, without suggesting that the dollar is the world's only currency or that gold still backs it.

**Format:** 1920×1080, 30 fps, approximately 60 seconds, original vector motion graphics, English narration (Kokoro `am_michael`, selected by user).

**Visual thesis:** A line of exchange becomes a gold anchor, breaks, then grows into a network. Each shot has one legible idea. A restrained editorial palette of near-black, warm ivory, gold, and dollar green; display type 72–130px; generous 120px safe margins; smooth seekable animations. Background uses fine drafting lines and subtle moving dots, not photographs. No Blender or unlicensed imagery.

**Continuity:** Timeline flows left-to-right; gold represents the Bretton Woods link; green represents dollar networks; broken gold line never reappears as a current backing claim. Words land before they are read. Transitions are short wipes and camera-scale moves, not strobing. Audio is the master; visual cuts follow each narration unit.

**Shot plan:**
1. Question / a dollar symbol emerges from exchange routes. Hook and visual thesis.
2. 1944 / the Bretton Woods name is printed as global currency nodes align to a dollar and gold bar.
3. 1971 / the gold connection splits; dollar remains on screen, shifts from gold to green.
4. Markets / a stack of Treasury tickets expands with rising liquidity bars.
5. Trade, lending, reserves / three independent connections link back to the dollar node.
6. Network effect / linked nodes propagate, with the same central dollar.
7. Limits / euro and yen enter; dominance is not exclusivity or permanence.
8. Closing / historical gold line becomes a living green network, and the answer resolves.

**QC:** Validate authored layout and browser errors, render actual MP4, inspect contact sheet and selected frames, test audio loudness, duration, streams, black/frozen frames and check no unsupported causal or historical claims. Any automated repair is capped at three.
